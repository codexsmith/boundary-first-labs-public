# Consequential and Institutional Systems

Public route for boundary-aware analysis of organizational processes, public institutions, economics, law, governance, and human-facing systems.

Use domain-native standards and distinguish description, normative judgment, procedural legality, verified evidence, and design proposal. Attribution, privacy, authorization, and consequences for affected people matter alongside formal coherence.

Public tools here are intended to support **evidenced explanation and inspectable plans**, not to make real-world decisions or intervene in institutions on a user's behalf. No general legal, economic, political, or medical conclusion follows solely from cross-domain formal analogies.

## Research highpoints — working, not released scientific results

### Viability Control Theory for Wicked Systems

**Status: candidate research framing, not a proved new control theory.** The Lab is examining whether viability-theoretic and control-theoretic tools can help reason about systems whose models, goals, authority, affected parties and constraints are contested, incomplete or changing.

Potential questions include which trajectories remain viable, how constraints change with evidence, when a repair reopens options, and whether an institution can appear viable only by transferring unsustainable burdens to individuals.

Prior work in viability theory, robust/control theory, wicked-problem research and human-centered systems engineering must be compared explicitly. A named framing is not evidence of a new theorem or demonstrated safety guarantee.

### Constructive Human Agency / Constructive Humanist Agentics

**Status: earlier working research/doctrine synthesis requiring current owner reconciliation.** The Lab has developed a constructive approach to agentic systems that aims to enlarge people's capacity to understand, choose, participate, refuse, contest, correct, cooperate and repair.

This includes the distinction between **originating, delegated, affected and repair agency**, and the proposition that computational capability does not create authority. The Lab's historical July 2026 synthesis is retained by its governed source owner; the public repository does not mirror or release that original artifact here.

### Why the two belong together

**Viability asks whether a system can continue within defensible constraints. Constructive agency asks who can act, refuse, contest, participate and repair within those constraints.** System persistence is not a substitute for human viability or legitimate agency.
