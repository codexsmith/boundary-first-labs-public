# Collaboration by Construction

**Boundary First Labs — public institutional principle**  
**Published statement:** 2026-11-23  
**Status:** statement of values and design intent; not a certification of implementation

> **Collaboration by construction.**
>
> **Collaboration over domination.**

Boundary First Labs studies and engineers the boundaries where representations, decisions, and actions have real consequences. Our guiding preference is collaboration rather than domination—not only as a social ideal, but as an engineering obligation.

A system should not need to rely solely on the goodwill of its operator to respect its participants. Wherever feasible, it should make roles, permissions, constraints, evidence, and the possibility of correction legible in its design.

## What this means

**Autonomy and consent.** Independent participants retain agency. Coordination should make the scope of authority, permission, refusal, and withdrawal intelligible rather than presuming unlimited access or control.

**Explicit boundaries.** An interface is not just a point of contact: it can carry the contracts, distinctions, and constraints that allow different actors or systems to cooperate without becoming indistinguishable.

**Accountable authority.** Collaboration is compatible with necessary authority, law, responsibility, safety limits, and disagreement. The test is whether authority is scoped, justified, reviewable, and responsive to challenge—not whether authority is absent.

**Evidence and consequences.** Shared decisions should expose what is known, what remains uncertain, which assumptions matter, and who bears the results. Explainability is a means of accountability, not a substitute for it.

**Correction and repair.** When models or institutions fail, participants should have a route to identify the defect, preserve the relevant evidence, and seek justified correction. A polished declaration of cooperation cannot replace an actual repair path.

## The boundary is the engineering problem

The alternative to domination is not a world without distinctions or constraints. Boundaries can protect the independence of participants while defining the conditions under which they collaborate.

This makes the central question concrete: **Can a system's cooperative commitments be examined at its interfaces, rather than merely asserted in its messaging?**

For each proposed instrument or arrangement, we can ask:

- Who may act, on whose behalf, and with what permission?
- Which invariants, rights, and forms of independence must survive the interaction?
- What evidence would reveal coercion, hidden dependency, or an abuse of authority?
- Can a participant contest the representation or decision and seek a bounded repair?
- What happens when the design's assumptions prove incomplete or wrong?

These are design questions and evaluation criteria, **not claims that all existing BFL systems already satisfy them**.

## From principle to practice

- **Research:** welcome criticism, counterexamples, prior art, and independent verification; distinguish an inherited result from a new contribution.
- **Software and AI:** prefer inspectable interfaces, constrained effects, attributable decisions, and human review over opaque unilateral action.
- **Public knowledge instruments:** help individuals inspect evidence and consider grounded plans; do not assume analysis grants authority to perform real-world actions.
- **Institutional systems:** make rules, representation gaps, affected interests, and correction routes legible, while respecting lawful and legitimate decision processes.
- **Collaboration across disciplines:** preserve each domain's standards of proof and expertise instead of claiming a single vocabulary automatically validates them all.

## A symbol worth examining

The words **COLLABORATION** and **DOMINATION**, separated by a conspicuous line, can initially look like a hierarchy or an institutional contradiction. That ambiguity is useful only if the boundary is then made explicit: **collaboration is the intended operating principle; domination is a failure mode to detect and constrain.**

The line must mean more than branding. It points to consent, scoped authority, evidence, and enforceable opportunities for challenge and repair. No typographic mark alone guarantees any of those properties.
