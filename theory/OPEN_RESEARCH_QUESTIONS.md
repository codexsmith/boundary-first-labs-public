# Boundary Theory — Questions Still Open

**Status:** bounded public research orientation, not a theorem docket or promise of resolution.

Boundary Theory investigates whether some recurring analytical roles—distinction, boundary, representation, transformation, admissibility, observation, defect, and repair—can be formalized in ways that remain useful when moving among mathematical and consequential domains.

A research question is not a validated answer.

## Five tests that matter

1. **Typed fidelity:** can a proposed comparison state which mathematical objects and operations it preserves, and which it loses?
2. **Reduction:** is the same result already explained adequately by native geometry, programming logic, category theory, control theory, formal methods, or existing domain standards?
3. **Nontrivial residual:** after comparison, is there a genuinely additional construction, relation, tool, or measurable practical advantage?
4. **Counterexamples:** can a suitable native test expose when the proposed transfer fails or becomes meaningless?
5. **Consequential adequacy:** do real-world uses bring additional physical, institutional, legal, safety, or evidentiary obligations that a formal analogy does not settle?

Our first public [n-ball calibration](../projects/n-ball-boundary-recursion/README.md) demonstrates the kind of separation needed between object-level geometry, its scalar measure, and a later operation. It does not answer these larger research questions.

## Ground rules

Classical mathematics stays classical. A new schema is not a newly discovered theorem. A successful program run is not scientific publication. Cross-domain similarity is not a universal ontology.

When the available domain-specific machinery suffices, it remains the correct primary tool.

The full private research apparatus has additional hypotheses, instruments, defects, and internal review obligations; this public note is not a comprehensive release of that work.
